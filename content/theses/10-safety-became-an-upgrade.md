## Context

Safety measures are often presented as universal responsibilities of a platform. In practice, screening and verification have been distributed unevenly between products and, later, packaged as optional features.

That division matters because users of a free service do not face a discounted version of physical harm. The risk remains real even when the account produces no subscription revenue.

## Argument

A joint investigation by ProPublica, Columbia Journalism Investigations and BuzzFeed News established that Match Group screened for registered sex offenders on the paid Match.com service and not on Tinder, OkCupid or Plenty of Fish. A company spokesperson, on the record: there are definitely registered sex offenders on our free products. Reporters reviewed more than 150 incidents of sexual assault connected to dating platforms over roughly a decade. A House Oversight subcommittee opened an inquiry in 2020. The company subsequently moved toward offering background checks as a paid feature.

Background checks are incomplete and cannot guarantee safety; absence from a registry is not proof of harmlessness. The argument is therefore not that one paid check solves abuse. It is that basic risk reduction was treated differently across a common portfolio, and additional reassurance could be positioned as a purchasable advantage rather than shared infrastructure.
